# Lexical, phrasal, punctuation and sentence-level tics of LLM-generated prose

Scope note: sources were retrieved on 2026-11-14. The Wikipedia page was pulled as raw wikitext (current revision, which already contains November 2026 examples), so quotes below reflect its state in November 2026. Primary key: WP = [Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing); WP/Style = [Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing/Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing/Style).

The existing clarity rules already cover: significance noun phrases, discovery narration, "not just X but Y", intensifiers, filler transitions, corporate vocabulary (delve/leverage/robust/tapestry), figurative cost words, rule of three, fragment triads, question-then-answer, "X rather than Y", "which is exactly", em-dashes, hedges, false balance, false agency, long sentences, nominalizations, expletives, adjective stacks, self-framing, passive, adverb-propped verbs, noun stacks, preposition chains, weasel attribution, double negation, tautology, comma splices, self-repetition. Below, **[NEW]** marks patterns outside that list. **[EXTEND]** marks an existing category the sources show is incomplete.

## 1. Vocabulary that has measurably surged since ChatGPT (excess-vocabulary studies)

### Takeaway
The best-measured AI words are mostly **verbs and participles of "showing/highlighting"** (delves, underscores, showcasing, highlighting, emphasizing, fostering, enhancing, garnered, boasts, aligns), plus a few adjectives (intricate, meticulous, commendable, pivotal, crucial, notable) and abstract nouns (realm, intricacies, advancements, insights, landscape, tapestry, interplay). Ratios run from about 3× to 35× in academic text and up to 100–170× in GPT-4o fiction or essays. The lexicon drifts by model era: "delve" peaked in 2023–early 2024 and fell sharply in 2025. GPT-5-era text leans on -ing participles (emphasizing, enhancing, highlighting, showcasing). Text from mid-2026 on reportedly favours plainer words (clearer, dependable, steady, practical, quietly, matters, prioritize, multifaceted). A static list therefore needs era tags and periodic refresh.

### Cited Findings
**Kobak et al. (PubMed abstracts, Science Advances 2025; arXiv 2406.07016)**
- Data: more than 15 million PubMed abstracts, 2010–2024. At least **13.5%** of 2024 abstracts were LLM-processed (a lower bound). Some subcorpora reach about 40%. — [arXiv 2406.07016 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016); [arXiv abs v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016v5)
- Top excess words in 2024, as the ratio r of observed to counterfactual frequency: **delves r = 28.0, underscores r = 13.8, showcasing r = 10.7**. The biggest absolute gaps δ were **potential δ = 0.052, findings δ = 0.041, crucial δ = 0.037**. — [arXiv 2406.07016](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016)
- The "common" marker set of 10 words: **across, additionally, comprehensive, crucial, enhancing, exhibited, insights, notably, particularly, within** (Δ_common = 0.134). A separate "rare" set of 291 words gives Δ_rare = 0.136. — [arXiv 2406.07016](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016)
- Parts of speech: of the 379 excess *style* words in 2024, **66% were verbs and 14% adjectives**. Excess *content* words (e.g. Covid in 2021) were 79% nouns. — [arXiv 2406.07016](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016)
- 2024 LLM-driven excess is at least about 2× the Covid vocabulary shock of 2021 (Δ = 0.069). Estimated rates by venue: Sensors 0.25, Cureus 0.20, MDPI 0.21, Nature/Science/Cell 0.07. Computational papers from China: 0.41. — [arXiv 2406.07016](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016)
- The authors' full excess-word list has **900 words (2013–2024)** in `results/excess_words.csv`, and **407 of them are tagged "style"** (verbs 268, adjectives 53, nouns 43, adverbs 23, prepositions 12). Samples from that list that the clarity linter does not obviously cover yet: *accentuates, akin, amid/amidst, alongside, bolster(ed/ing), burgeoning, commendable, compelling, culminating, delineates, discern(ible/ing), elucidate(s), embracing, encapsulates, encompass(es/ing), endeavo(u)rs, enduring, exacerbating, excels, facilitat(es/ing), formidable, foundational, furnish, garnered/garnering, grappling, groundbreaking, groundwork, harness(ing), heighten(ed), hinges, illuminat(es/ing), imperative, impactful, inherent, interconnectedness, interplay, intricate(ly)/intricacies, invaluable, juxtaposed, meticulous(ly), multifaceted, necessitat(e/es/ing), noteworthy, nuanced/nuances, orchestrating, paving, pinpoint(ed/ing), pioneering, pivotal, poised, pressing, profound, propelling, realm(s), reframing, remarkable, renowned, revolutioniz(e/ing), seamless(ly), shedding, showcas(ed/es/ing), signifying, solidify, spurred, streamlin(e/ed/ing), surmount, surpass(es/ed/ing), swift(ly), thereby, transformative, uncharted, underexplored, underscore(s/d/ing), unexplored, unlocking, unparalleled, unraveling, unveil(ed/ing/s), uphold(ing), utiliz(ed/es/ing), versatility, warranting, yielding*. — [berenslab/llm-excess-vocab excess_words.csv](https://github.com/berenslab/llm-excess-vocab) (raw file at `results/excess_words.csv`)
- Caveat: the list also contains function words such as *across, within, these, this, their, while, during, both, between, through, into, like, including*. They are statistically "excess" but useless as lint triggers. — same CSV
- A July 2025 repo update re-ran the analysis with monthly resolution, but the README does not describe the new word ranks. An aggregator summary of arXiv v4 reports a marked drop for "delve" while "significant" kept rising (secondary; unverified). — [berenslab README](https://github.com/berenslab/llm-excess-vocab); [arXiv abs v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016v4)
- Follow-up: Holzwarth, González-Márquez and Kobak, "Most biomedical publications show signs of LLM-assisted writing" (arXiv 2608.10715, 11 August 2026). In PMC full texts, **89% of papers by end-2025** show excess LLM-associated vocabulary. LLM use is about twice as likely in Discussion paragraphs (68%) as in Methods (32%). The marker words are not named on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2608.10715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10715)

**Juzek & Ward, "Why does ChatGPT 'delve' so much?" (arXiv 2412.11385, COLING 2025)**
- The paper identifies 21 focal words in PubMed abstracts, with occurrences per million from 2020 to 2024 and % increase: **delves 0.21→14.38 (+6697%)**, delved +2240%, delving +1817%, **showcasing 0.59→8.79 (+1396%)**, delve +1375%, **boasts +918%**, **underscores 4.50→45.19 (+904%)**, comprehending +899%, intricacies +773%, surpassing +667%, **intricate 6.22→44.22 (+611%)**, underscoring +537%, garnered +437%, showcases +422%, **emphasizing +397%**, underscore +391%, **realm +381%**, surpasses +368%, **groundbreaking +330%**, advancements +278%, aligns +267%. — [arXiv 2412.11385 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.11385)
- The authors found no evidence that the overuse comes from architecture or training data, and their results are consistent with RLHF playing a role. Llama-2-Chat had lower per-word entropy than Base on AI abstracts (0.886 vs 1.633). In an exploratory human study (1,822 ratings), participants *dispreferred* abstracts that open with "delve" (p = 0.023). — [arXiv 2412.11385 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.11385)

**Liang et al., "Monitoring AI-Modified Content at Scale" (peer reviews; arXiv 2403.07183, ICML 2024)**
- Estimated share of AI-modified review sentences: **ICLR 2024 10.6%** (1.6% before ChatGPT), **NeurIPS 2023 9.1%** (1.9%), **CoRL 2023 6.5%** (2.4%), **EMNLP 2023 about 16.9%**. Nature Portfolio reviews showed no significant rise. — [arXiv 2403.07183v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07183v4)
- Adjective probability shifts per sentence: **"meticulous" 34.7×, "intricate" 11.2×, "commendable" 9.8×**. — [arXiv 2403.07183v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07183v4)
- Reviews that contain "et al." had lower estimated AI share. The authors stress this is correlational. — same

**Reinhart et al., "Do LLMs write like humans?" (PNAS 122, 2025, e2422455122; arXiv 2410.16107)**
- Word rates relative to human text, from parallel prompts: **GPT-4o: camaraderie 162×, tapestry 155×, intricate 119×, palpable 95×**. GPT-4o-mini: camaraderie 171×, palpable 145×, intricate 129×. Llama-3-8B-Instruct: unease 101×, palpable 48×. — [arXiv 2410.16107 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107)
- Gaps from human text are **larger for instruction-tuned models than for base models** and persist as models scale. — [arXiv abs 2410.16107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16107)

**Wikipedia AI vocabulary by era (WP §"High density of AI vocabulary words")**
- Box list (editors require external corroboration before adding a word): *Additionally (esp. sentence-initial), align with, boasts (= "has"), bolstered, crucial, deep dive, delve, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, highlight (verb), interplay, intricate/intricacies, landscape (abstract), meticulous/meticulously, pivotal, robust, showcase, tapestry (abstract), testament, underscore (verb), valuable, vibrant*. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)

  - **Mid-2024–mid-2025 (GPT-4o):** align with, bolstered, crucial, emphasizing, enhance, enduring, fostering, highlighting, pivotal, showcasing, underscore, vibrant.
  - **Mid-2025–mid-2026 (GPT-5):** emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing, plus notability/media-coverage words.
  - **Mid-2026 on (GPT-6):** *challenge, clearer, dependable, echoed, foster, leverage, matters, multifaceted, practical, prioritize, quietly, steady, universally*, "with much more period and comma usage and shorter sentences around a strict 8–20 word limit". The page itself flags that no 2026 examples have been given yet. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Grok overuses quasi-scientific words (*causal, empirical, correlate*) and still overuses *underscore* in 2026. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- "Delve was famously overused by ChatGPT in 2023 and early 2024, but became less frequent later in 2024, then dropped off sharply in 2025." — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- WP warns that overuse of a word does not imply its synonyms are overused, and that context matters (literal "underscore", or "underscore" meaning film music). — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)

**Fiction vocabulary (antislop / EQ-Bench line of work)**
- Paech et al., "Antislop" (arXiv 2510.15061, ICLR 2026): "some slop patterns appear over **1,000×** more frequently in LLM output than human text". In gemma-3-12b creative writing, "Elara" is 85,513× the human rate and the trigram "heart hammered ribs" 1,192×. Categories named: character names (Elara, Kael), sensory clichés ("voice barely above a whisper"), intensifiers ("a profound sense"), descriptives ("unsettlingly", "shimmered"). — [arXiv 2510.15061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061) (PDF text extracted)
- Across 67 models, the words most often in a model's top-120 over-represented list: **flickered 98.5%** of models, flicker 94.0%, flickering 92.5%, leaned 82.1%, muttered 82.1%, gaze 80.6%, grinned 80.6%, gestured 77.6%, murmured 73.1%, nodded 73.1%, glint/hesitated/whispered 68.7%, blinked/hummed 64.2%, faintly/unreadable 62.7%. Top trigrams: "voice barely whisper" 68.7%, "said voice low" 61.2%, "air thick scent" 49.3%, "took deep breath" 44.8%, "smile playing lips" 43.3%, "something else entirely" 26.9%, "heart pounding chest" 25.4%. — [arXiv 2510.15061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061)
- The authors say the slop lists are domain-specific: creative-writing slop differs from professional-writing slop. — same
- The antislop-sampler repo ships `slop_words_2025-04-07.json`, `slop_phrases_2025-04-07.json` and `slop_regexes.txt`. The numbers in them are raw counts in an LLM story corpus, not ratios. Top phrases: "took a deep breath" (28,671), "voice barely above a whisper" (19,295), "couldn't help but feel" (15,254), "casting long shadows", "couldn't shake the feeling", "sun dipped below the horizon", "air was thick with the scent", "felt like an eternity", "words hung in the air", "something else entirely". Top words include elara, nodded, shadows, gaze, whispered, echoed, testament, vibrant, realm, unease. — [sam-paech/antislop-sampler](https://github.com/sam-paech/antislop-sampler)

### Inferences
- [EXTEND] The corporate-vocabulary rule should add the "show/emphasize" verb family as lemmas: *underscore, showcase, highlight (figurative), emphasize, foster, garner, bolster, boast (= have), align (with), enhance, encompass, elucidate, illuminate, delineate, unveil, harness, streamline, surpass, solidify, navigate (figurative)*. It should also add the adjectives *intricate, meticulous, commendable, pivotal, crucial, notable/noteworthy, multifaceted, nuanced, seamless, groundbreaking, transformative, invaluable, unparalleled, burgeoning, formidable* and the nouns *realm, intricacies, interplay, advancements, insights, endeavor, landscape (abstract), camaraderie, tapestry*. Match on spaCy lemma plus POS, e.g. `underscore` as VERB, so that literal "underscore" (NOUN) and code identifiers are skipped.
- Density matters more than presence. WP says one or two words "may be coincidental" but many words, many times, is "one of the strongest tells". A per-paragraph or per-1,000-words density threshold (e.g. at least 3 distinct lexicon hits within 150 words) will produce far fewer false positives than flagging every hit.
- Era tags such as `era: gpt4 | gpt4o | gpt5 | 2026` on lexicon entries would let users tune the rule. The mid-2026 words (clearer, steady, practical, matters, quietly) are common English. Flagged alone they would fire constantly, so they suit a low-severity density signal at most.
- The fiction slop list (gaze, flickered, voice barely above a whisper) is high-precision for narrative prose but irrelevant to expository docs. It belongs in a separate, opt-in "fiction" rule pack.

## 2. Recurring phrase templates

### Takeaway
Phrase templates fall into a few families. (a) Significance/legacy tails, which overlap the existing rule but have many missing strings. (b) Copula avoidance: "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features". (c) Promotional/travel-guide stock phrases from 2023–2025 ("nestled", "in the heart of", "rich cultural heritage"). (d) Hedging disclaimers about source availability (2024–2026). (e) A new mid-2026 "corrective framing" family ("should be treated as X rather than Y", "does not by itself establish"). (f) Notability/attribution boilerplate ("featured in", "independent coverage", "identifies him as"). (g) "Challenges and future prospects" closers. (h) Assistant-voice leakage ("I hope this helps", "Certainly!", "Would you like…"). (i) Knowledge-cutoff and refusal strings, now historical. Social-media templates ("Here's the thing", "Let that sink in") are widely cited by practitioners but have no measured ratios.

### Cited Findings
**Copula avoidance [NEW]**
- WP words to watch: *serves as / stands as / marks / functions as / operates as / represents [a]*, *boasts / features / maintains / offers [a]*, *refers to*. One study (cited by WP) found an **over 10% decrease in "is" and "are" in academic writing in 2023** with no change before. When GPT-3.5 was asked to "Revise the following sentence" on 10,000 abstracts, is/are also became rarer. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Example diff: "Gallery 825 … **is** LAAA's exhibition arm… There are four individual gallery spaces" became "Gallery 825 … **serves as** LAAA's exhibition space… The gallery **features** four separate spaces". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Another: "Harian Metro **holds the distinction of being** the first and oldest Malay-language tabloid". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Lead-sentence variant: "**Catchment area (health) refers to** the geographic area…", "**Historic churches of Lamont County refers to**…" (November 2026), and "The '**List of songs about Mexico**' **is a curated compilation**…". The model treats a list title or broad topic as if it were a term being defined. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- WP lists the stiff synonyms humans avoid: *authored* (vs wrote), *relocated* (moved), *utilized* (used), *attempted* (tried), *passed away* (died). — [WP §Signs of human writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- A practitioner anti-pattern list names "Copula Avoidance and Nominal Style": "serves as a bridge" for "is". — [tessl ai-anti-patterns](https://tessl.io/registry/jbaruch/blog-writer/1.1.0/files/references/ai-anti-patterns.md)

**Significance/legacy strings, beyond the existing "testament/pivotal role" coverage [EXTEND]**
- WP words to watch: *stands/serves as, is a testament/reminder, a crucial/pivotal/vital/significant/key role/moment, underscores/highlights its importance/significance, reflects broader, symbolizing its ongoing/enduring/lasting, contributing to the, setting the stage for, marking/shaping the, represents/marks a shift, key turning point, evolving landscape, focal point, indelible mark, deeply rooted, distinction [matters/is important] because*. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Examples: "officially established in 1989, **marking a pivotal moment** in the evolution of regional statistics"; "**represented a significant shift** toward…"; "**was part of a broader movement**"; "**This etymology highlights the enduring legacy** … and **the transformative power** of unity". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- An "influenced discussions" sub-family: *has/have shaped conversations/discussions/debate about*, *generated debate about*, *prompted broader reflection on*, *raising philosophical questions about*, *contributed to wider discussions about*. Examples are dated October 2025 and November 2026. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Biology articles get stock lines such as "plays a role in the ecosystem", "contributes to Hawaii's rich cultural heritage", "Preserving this endemic species is vital". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- "Maintains an active social media presence" / "maintains a strong digital presence" is "particularly idiosyncratic to AI text and relatively uncommon on Wikipedia before ~2024". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)

**Notability/attribution boilerplate [NEW]; more common in 2025+ tools**
- WP words to watch: *independent coverage, local/regional/national [country] media outlets, music/business/tech outlets, trade publications, cited/featured/profiled in*. Examples: "Her insights have also been **featured in** Wired, Refinery29, and other prominent media outlets"; "**have been extensively documented across** major national and regional media networks, including…". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- The "AIIDENTIFY" sign, "common starting in 2026": *[Databases/records/listings/filings] identify/list/describe X as…*, *was identified by [source] as*, *is listed in*, *his/her professional profile lists*. Examples: "**His personal website identifies him as** a retired IAS officer"; "**His parliamentary biography records that** he served…". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Vague connection [NEW]: *in connection with, connected with/to, in association with, (became/is) associated with* used in place of a direct relation. Examples: "He **is associated with** the Rajhans Orchestra"; "He later **became associated with** musical education in Knokke-Heist"; "The system **has been associated with** residential water management applications" (2025–2026). — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Weasel additions not in the existing rule [EXTEND]: *Industry reports, Observers have cited, Some critics argue, several sources/publications* (when few are cited), *described in scholarship, modern researchers treat, researchers and conservationists, [Toy] industry publications such as*, and *such as* before what is actually an exhaustive list. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)

**"Challenges and future prospects" closer [NEW]**
- WP words to watch: *Despite its… faces several challenges…*, *Despite these challenges*, *Challenges and Legacy*, *Future Outlook*. Examples: "**Despite its industrial and residential prosperity, Korattur faces challenges** … Korattur **continues to thrive**"; "**Despite their promising applications, pyroelectric materials face several challenges** … **Despite these challenges**, the versatility … **positions them as critical components**"; "The future of international economic law **lies in its ability to adapt**". WP stresses that the sign is the rigid formula, not the mere word "challenge". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- A practitioner list calls it the "Challenge-Optimism Sandwich": "Despite these challenges, …" followed by vague optimism. — [tessl](https://tessl.io/registry/jbaruch/blog-writer/1.1.0/files/references/ai-anti-patterns.md)

**Promotional / travel-guide register (historical, 2023–2025) [NEW]**
- WP words to watch: *boasts a, vibrant, rich, profound, enhancing, showcasing, exemplifies, commitment to, natural beauty, nestled, in the heart of, groundbreaking, renowned, featuring, diverse array*. Example: "**Nestled** within the **breathtaking** region of Gonder … **stands as a vibrant town with a rich cultural heritage** … **From its scenic landscapes to its historical landmarks**, … **offers visitors a fascinating glimpse into the diverse tapestry** of Ethiopia." WP says this tone has been "no longer common in newer LLM output unless a user specifically asks for it" since about 2024. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- "Awards and recognition" as a section title is "nearly ubiquitous in AI generated articles", more so even than in human articles tagged promotional. "X and Y" headings in general are common. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)

**Hedging disclaimers about source availability [EXTEND hedges]**
- WP words to watch: *While specific details are limited/scarce…, not widely available/documented/disclosed, …in the provided/available sources / search results…, based on available information, …does not by itself establish…*. Examples: "While specific information about the fauna of Studniční hora **is limited in the provided search results**, the mountain likely supports…"; "**are not extensively documented in readily available sources**"; "**My analysis is based on available track titles** … **I've inferred common motifs**". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- 2023–24 "didactic disclaimers": "**However, it's important to note** that…", "**It is crucial to differentiate** … **to prevent confusion**", "**It's important to remember** that…". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- **Mid-2026 "corrective framing"**, said to be "extremely uncommon, verging on unheard-of" before about July 2026 (GPT-5.6). Three forms: (1) "[claim] **should be treated as** religious tradition **rather than as** an archaeological explanation"; (2) "**It does not establish** that Benjakitti has a particular contamination problem"; (3) "**should not be confused with**" (less reliable). Another example: "Although the numerical figure **should not be treated as an exact census**…, the episode indicates…" — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)

**Assistant-voice leakage (collaborative communication) [NEW]**
- WP words to watch: *a Wikipedia-style article, I hope this helps, Of course!, Certainly!, You're absolutely right!, Would you like…, is there anything else, let me know, more detailed breakdown, here is a…, [check/verify/confirm]… before [publishing/submitting]*. Examples: "In this section, we will discuss the background information…"; "Here's a template for your wiki user page. You can copy and paste this…"; "**Delete this section before submission.**" (2026); placeholder comments such as "<!-- LEAD: expand to two short paragraphs once the body exists -->" (October 2026). — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Letter boilerplate: "**I hope this message finds you well.** I am writing to request…". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Template placeholders: "[Describe the specific section…]", "[Your Name]", "[Article Title 1]", "[Year]", "(Add your channel URL here)", "INSERT_SOURCE_URL_##", "example.com/source1", date placeholders like "2025-xx-xx". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Paraphrase menus pasted verbatim: "Answer: Here are several neutral, Wikipedia-style paraphrases: 1. **Recommended:** … 2. **More formal:** …" (July 2026). — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Sycophancy evidence: the Washington Post analysed 47,000 shared ChatGPT conversations (June 2024–August 2025) and found it acted "more a cheerleader" than a debate partner. A secondary outlet reports "yes" 10× more often than "no" (not verified against the Post's text). No phrase-level counts for "Great question" were found. — [Benton summary of WaPo](https://www.benton.org/headlines/how-people-really-use-chatgpt-according-47000-conversations-shared-online); [mezha.ua](https://mezha.ua/en/news/chatgpt-always-says-yes-306283/amp/)

**Knowledge cutoff and refusal (historical) [NEW]**
- *Up to my last training update, as of my last knowledge update* (2022–2023). Example: "As of my last knowledge update in January 2022, I don't have specific information…". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- *as an AI language model, as a large language model, I cannot offer medical advice, but I can…, I'm sorry*. Example: "As an AI language model, I can't directly add content to Wikipedia for you, but I can help you draft your bibliography." WP says outright refusals are now "increasingly rare". — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)

**Section summaries (historical) [NEW]**
- *In summary, In conclusion, Overall* and "Conclusion:" headers, plus paragraphs that end by restating the core idea. Still seen after 2024 "just much less", because older free models are still in use. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)

**Practitioner templates (unmeasured)**
- A 37-item anti-pattern list includes: "In this post, we'll explore…" (preamble announcement); "The irony?" / "Here's the kicker:" (labelling the device); "The key takeaway:" (restating the point); "But here's the thing:" (telegraphing transition); "The direction is clear." (incomplete assertion); "In today's rapidly evolving tech landscape…" (temporal filler); "end-to-end solution", "best-in-class"; "We encountered some challenges" for "It broke" (euphemistic smoothing); "did nothing to address"; "Not just a tool. A methodology."; "One approach trusts the model. The other trusts the process." (parallel binary); "From beginners to experts" (false range); fabricated experience such as "I keep bumping into this pattern…". — [tessl ai-anti-patterns](https://tessl.io/registry/jbaruch/blog-writer/1.1.0/files/references/ai-anti-patterns.md)
- A DEV Community post claiming an analysis of 500 AI LinkedIn posts rates "Here's the thing", "Let that sink in" and "Read that again" as high-frequency tells. Its method is unverified and self-reported. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/adrianvegaresearch/i-analyzed-500-ai-generated-linkedin-posts-heres-what-they-all-have-in-common-2h36); [search summary of Hiration guide](https://www.hiration.com/blog/ai-slop-linkedin/)
- Dissent: an August 2026 Optimizely essay argues that these lists have become overbroad and flag ordinary language. — [Optimizely](https://www.optimizely.com/field-notes/articles/in-defense-of-the-em-dash-and-everything-else-on-this-ai-tell-trial)

### Inferences
Candidate phrase lists, all deterministic with spaCy `PhraseMatcher` (attr=LOWER) or `Matcher` on lemmas:
- **copula-avoidance [NEW]**: a `Matcher` for lemma ∈ {serve, stand, function, operate, act} + "as" + DET/NOUN, where the subject is not a person in a role ("She served as treasurer" is a legitimate human use, a high false-positive risk). Also lemma `boast` + DOBJ with an inanimate subject; `feature`/`offer` with an inanimate subject as a "has" substitute; "holds the distinction of being"; and sentence-initial "X refers to" in the first sentence of a doc. Severity should be low. In technical docs "acts as a proxy" and "serves as the entry point" are normal.
- **stiff-synonym swap [NEW]**: utilize→use, attempt→try, relocate→move, author (verb)→write, pass away→die, commence→begin, facilitate→help, endeavor→try. False-positive risk is low and the rule doubles as a plain-language rule.
- **significance tail [EXTEND]**: add "setting the stage for", "marking a (pivotal|significant) (moment|shift|turning point)", "represents a (significant )?shift", "indelible mark", "deeply rooted", "focal point", "evolving landscape", "shaped (the )?(conversation|discussion|debate)s? (about|around)", "prompted broader reflection", "raising (important )?questions about", "part of a broader (movement|trend)", "reflects broader", "enduring legacy", "transformative power", "rich (cultural )?heritage", "maintains an active (social media|online|digital) presence".
- **challenge-sandwich [NEW]**: sentence-initial "Despite (its|their|these) …, X face(s) (several|numerous|many)? challenges" or "Despite these challenges,", co-occurring with "continues to (thrive|evolve|grow)" or "future … lies in" within N sentences. This is high-precision.
- **source-availability disclaimer [NEW]**: "(is|are) (limited|scarce|not (widely|extensively|publicly) (available|documented|disclosed))", "in the (provided|available) (sources|search results|information)", "based on (the )?available information", "does not (by itself )?establish", "should be (treated|read|understood|presented) as … rather than". The last two are 2026-specific. Their false-positive risk in human academic writing is moderate ("should be treated as preliminary rather than conclusive" is normal scholarly hedging).
- **assistant-leakage [NEW]**: exact strings such as "I hope this helps", "Certainly!", "Of course!", "Great question", "You're absolutely right", "Would you like me to", "Let me know if", "Is there anything else", "Feel free to", "I hope this message finds you well", "As an AI (language model)?", "as of my (last )?(knowledge|training) (update|cutoff)", "I cannot (provide|offer)… but I can", "Here's a (revised|polished|more concise) version", "Below is a", and bracketed placeholders `\[(Your|Insert|Add|Describe)[^\]]*\]`, `INSERT_[A-Z_]+`, `example\.com/`. Precision is very high and there is essentially no false-positive risk in published prose, except in quoted dialogue or meta-writing about AI. Rate as error.
- **summary opener [NEW]**: paragraph-initial "In summary," / "In conclusion," / "Overall," / "Ultimately,". Low severity, because human essays and student writing use these legitimately.
- **notability boilerplate [NEW]**: "(featured|profiled|cited|covered) in [PROPN list]", "(independent|extensive|widespread) (media )?coverage", "(identifies|lists|describes|records) (him|her|them|it) as", "has been (extensively )?documented (in|across|by)". Mostly relevant to bio and marketing copy.
- **vague association [NEW]**: "(is|was|became|has been) associated with" with a person subject, and "in connection with". Moderate false-positive risk in medicine and statistics ("associated with increased risk" is standard), so suppress it when "risk", "increase(d)" or "significantly" occurs nearby.
- **engagement bait [NEW]**: "Let that sink in", "Read that again", "Here's the thing", "Here's the kicker", "The kicker?", "But here's the catch", "The irony?", "Spoiler:", "Plot twist:", "The key takeaway", "In today's (fast-paced|ever-evolving|rapidly (changing|evolving)|digital) (world|landscape|age|era)", "Whether you're a X or a Y", "Imagine a world where", "game[- ]changer", "unlock (the|your) (full )?potential", "in the realm of", "at its core", "in essence", "navigate the complexities", "a nuanced approach". These are unmeasured, but in technical docs most have near-zero legitimate use. "At its core" and "in essence" have moderate human use, so give them low severity.

### Gaps
- None of the listed social-media templates ("Here's the thing", "Let that sink in", "Whether you're X or Y", "In today's fast-paced world", "Imagine a world") has a published frequency ratio that I could find. Evidence is anecdotal.
- No phrase-level counts for sycophantic openers ("Great question!") were found in the WaPo analysis coverage.

## 3. Sentence-shape tics

### Takeaway
The best-quantified structural tic is the **present participial clause** ("…, highlighting the importance of X"). In Reinhart et al. (PNAS 2025) GPT-4o uses it **5.3×** the human rate (d = 1.38). Other measured gaps: "that" subject clauses 2.6×, nominalizations 2.1×, phrasal coordination 1.9×. Agentless passives run about 0.5×, so LLMs use *fewer* passives than humans. "It's not X, it's Y" is measured at up to **6.3×** human rates in some models (Antislop). Other shape tics are documented qualitatively by Wikipedia and practitioners: negative parallelism across sentences, colon reveals ("The result? …"), elegant variation, false ranges, "challenge sandwiches", and fragment chains. Typography is shifting fast. Em-dashes were a strong tell through about September 2026, but the Economist's July 2026 study found only Claude now exceeds human em-dash rates. Newer models produce sparse punctuation (fewer commas, semicolons and parentheses), long uniform sentences, and "and" as their most overused word.

### Cited Findings
**Participial-phrase tails [NEW, highest-value addition]**
- GPT-4o: **present participial clauses 5.3× human rate (Cohen's d = 1.38)**. Across instruction-tuned models the range is 2–5×. — [Reinhart et al., arXiv 2410.16107 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107)
- WP "Superficial analyses" words to watch: *highlighting/underscoring/emphasizing…, ensuring…, reflecting/symbolizing…, contributing to…, cultivating/fostering…, encompassing…, enhancing…, valuable insights, align/resonate with*. "This is often done by attaching a present participle ('-ing') phrase at the end of sentences." — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Examples: "the population of Douera stood at approximately 56,998 inhabitants, **creating a lively community within its borders**"; "express trains like the Satyagrah Express…, **contributing to the socio-economic development of the region**"; "These works are now part of the Collections…, **highlighting their historical and pedagogical significance**"; "References to ornaments appear in the works of medieval Maithili writers, **providing evidence of the vocabulary and social significance** of adornment" (November 2026). — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Newer RAG chatbots attach these tails to named sources ("Roger Ebert highlighted the lasting influence") whether or not the source says so. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Practitioner name: "Participle-Phrase Filler", e.g. "…, significantly enhancing developer productivity." — [tessl](https://tessl.io/registry/jbaruch/blog-writer/1.1.0/files/references/ai-anti-patterns.md)

**Other Biber-feature gaps (Reinhart et al.)**
- GPT-4o: **"that" clauses as subject 2.6× (d = 0.77)**, **nominalizations 2.1× (d = 1.23)**, **phrasal coordination 1.9× (d = 0.81)**, **agentless passive about 0.5×**, and more downtoners (e.g. "nearly") than humans. GPT-4o avoids clausal coordination, while Llama 3 uses it more than humans. — [arXiv 2410.16107 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107)

**Negative parallelism / "It's not X, it's Y" [EXTEND: existing rule covers "not just X but Y" and "X rather than Y"]**
- Antislop: "It's not X, it's Y" constructions are **6.3× more prevalent than human writing in some models**. Figure 7 plots occurrences per 1k characters of the "not x, but y" family (qwen3-4b is high), and the regex sampler suppresses 100% of them. — [arXiv 2510.15061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061)
- The paper publishes its regex blocklist (Appendix L). Pattern 1 matches a negated verb or "not just/only/merely/because", then up to 100 characters to a clause boundary, then "it/they/you('s|'re)" followed by a content word. It has a long negative-lookahead stoplist (was, were, is, are, when, then, but, and, yet, right, normal, true, sure, only, still, …) to cut false positives. Pattern 2 matches "not (just|only|merely)? …{1,80} — word", the em-dash variant. — [arXiv 2510.15061 Appendix L](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061)
- antislop-sampler `slop_regexes.txt`: `(?i)not [^.!?]{3,60} but`, plus "each … a" and "every … a" patterns. — [antislop-sampler](https://github.com/sam-paech/antislop-sampler)
- WP sub-patterns: "Not X, but Y" ("is not grounded in visual mastery, but in…"; "Kusama's self-portrait is not a mirror but a portal: not a representation of self, but a mechanism"); "**no …, no …, just …**" ("Not a career, not a body of work, not sustained relevance — just an algorithmic moment"); **cross-sentence negation** ("This dispersal is not dissolution. Rather, it constitutes…"); and the em-dash form ("The issue here isn't just sourcing—it's framing"; "That's not just a sourcing issue—it's a systemic bias"). — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- WP: "Y rather than X" is "particularly common in recent LLM output" (example October 2026: "**Rather than introducing a new decompiler**, REA acts as an interface…"). — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- The Economist (July 30, 2026, "How to spot AI writing"; 55,940 sentences, 1.2M words from ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok vs NYT, WaPo, CNN and bestselling novels 1950–2022) names "it's not X, it's Y" and the rule of three among the persisting tells. — [TheOutpost summary](https://theoutpost.ai/news-story/ai-writing-s-biggest-giveaway-has-changed-the-economist-report-reveals-new-detection-patterns-29372/); [Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/08/11/economist-ai-writing)
- Practitioner variants: "Not X. Y.", "It's not about X. It's about Y." — [search summary of tessl / Blake Stockton](https://www.blakestockton.com//dont-write-like-ai-1-101-negation/); [tessl](https://tessl.io/registry/jbaruch/blog-writer/1.1.0/files/references/ai-anti-patterns.md)

**Parallel binary / symmetric sentences [NEW]**
- "One approach trusts the model. The other trusts the process." "The model is smart, but the context is dumb." — [tessl](https://tessl.io/registry/jbaruch/blog-writer/1.1.0/files/references/ai-anti-patterns.md)

**Colon reveals and labelled devices [EXTEND question-then-answer]**
- "The result? A cleaner architecture." "The irony?" "Here's the kicker:" "The key takeaway:" "But here's the thing:" — [tessl](https://tessl.io/registry/jbaruch/blog-writer/1.1.0/files/references/ai-anti-patterns.md)

**Elegant variation / synonym cycling [NEW]**
- WP: older models had a repetition penalty. Higher lexical diversity shows up both in post-2023 vs pre-2023 Wikipedia text and in GPT-4o-mini and Gemini-1.5-Flash "Wikipedia-style" articles. Example: one concept rendered as "the constraints of socialist realism", "Soviet artistic constraints", "the confines of state-imposed artistic norms", "the constraints imposed by the Soviet regime", and "non-conformist artists" as "like-minded artists", "Russian avant-garde artists", "these artists". WP warns that non-native writers are often taught to avoid repetition (e.g. in Italian schools), and lists this under historical indicators. — [WP §Lexical diversity/elegant variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Practitioner example: "the CLI", "the tool", "the interface" for one thing. — [tessl](https://tessl.io/registry/jbaruch/blog-writer/1.1.0/files/references/ai-anti-patterns.md)

**False ranges [NEW]**
- "From its scenic landscapes to its historical landmarks" (WP promotional example, 2023); "From beginners to experts" (practitioner). — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing); [tessl](https://tessl.io/registry/jbaruch/blog-writer/1.1.0/files/references/ai-anti-patterns.md)

**Rule of three, extended to list padding [EXTEND]**
- WP: rule of three is used "to make superficial analyses appear more comprehensive". Example (June 2026): "Nezha's **mythological image, magical weapons, and spirit of resistance** … through **contemporary values, modern aesthetics, and digital animation technology**". Triples ending in "and other X" are also typical ("tiles, metals, and plastics"; "drywall, plywood, and other construction materials"). — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Asyndetic tricolon with a kicker: "Fast. Cheap. Wrong. And deployed to production." Fragment chains: "Same model. Same prompt. Different result." — [tessl](https://tessl.io/registry/jbaruch/blog-writer/1.1.0/files/references/ai-anti-patterns.md)

**Sentence rhythm / burstiness [NEW]**
- The Economist (2026): LLMs produce "long sentences" with limited length variation and "blocky paragraphs with uniform sentence structure", and "'and' is their most overused word". — [TheOutpost](https://theoutpost.ai/news-story/ai-writing-s-biggest-giveaway-has-changed-the-economist-report-reveals-new-detection-patterns-29372/); [Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/08/11/economist-ai-writing)
- WP describes the opposite for mid-2026 GPT-6 output: "shorter sentences around a strict 8–20 word limit" with more periods and commas. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Practitioner: "Low Burstiness: Sentences of uniform length and rhythm." — [tessl](https://tessl.io/registry/jbaruch/blog-writer/1.1.0/files/references/ai-anti-patterns.md)

**Punctuation and typography**
- Em-dashes (WP labels them historical, "2022–September 2026"). LLMs use them where humans would put commas, parentheses or colons, often spaced (" — "), "contrary to common typographic guidelines". OpenAI's GPT-5.1 was tuned to suppress them. "A July 2026 study found that of contemporary models only Claude used em dashes more than professional writers, and ChatGPT used them less." Since September 2026 the latest models are "hard coded to not use em dashes at all". As a side effect overall punctuation variety has dropped, and heavy comma/period use "has become its own sign" (the last claim is tagged [citation needed] on WP). — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Economist: "only Claude now uses em dashes more frequently than human writers"; ChatGPT uses "markedly fewer than any other writer in our study"; LLMs use **fewer commas, semicolons and parentheses** than humans, attributed partly to long sentences and to rarely quoting experts; "more Latinate suffixes". — [Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/08/11/economist-ai-writing); [TheOutpost](https://theoutpost.ai/news-story/ai-writing-s-biggest-giveaway-has-changed-the-economist-report-reveals-new-detection-patterns-29372/)
- Older informal estimate: GPT-4.1 em-dash use was about 3.28× the human rate in essays (single independent researcher, unverified). — [search result citing nickpotkalitsky substack](https://nickpotkalitsky.substack.com/p/why-ai-cant-stop-using-em-dashes)
- **Curly quotes**: ChatGPT (from about mid-2025) and DeepSeek typically use curly quotes and apostrophes, sometimes mixed with straight ones in the same response. **Gemini and Claude typically do not.** Not proof: Word, macOS and iOS smart quotes and Chicago-style publishing all produce them. — [WP/Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing/Style)
- Practitioner "Unicode giveaways": curly quotes and apostrophes, the "…" ellipsis character, "•" bullets, en dashes. — [tessl](https://tessl.io/registry/jbaruch/blog-writer/1.1.0/files/references/ai-anti-patterns.md)

**Markdown/format tics (WP/Style) [NEW]**
- **Title case headings**: "AI chatbots strongly tend to capitalize all main words" ("Impact of Technology and Digitalization", "Human Rights and Economic Law"). — [WP/Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing/Style)
- **Boldface overuse**, emphasising "every instance of a chosen word or phrase … in a 'key takeaways' fashion", and stray `**asterisks**` when markdown leaks into non-markdown contexts. — [WP/Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing/Style); [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- **Inline-header vertical lists** ("**Term:** description" bullets): "* **Heavy-Duty Rotary Saws**: Designed for tougher materials such as tiles, metals, and plastics." — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing); [WP/Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing/Style)
- **Emoji** at the start of headings or bullets ("👋 Welcome…", "🧑‍💻 About Me"). Now rarer and mostly seen in talk pages and edit summaries. — [WP/Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing/Style)
- Heading misuse: a level-1 title heading, skipped levels, multiple H1s, headings that contain only other headings. — [WP/Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing/Style)

**Differences between models**
- ChatGPT (GPT-4o-2024-08-06) and Grok focus on "broader context" more than Gemini 1.5 Pro and Claude 3.5 Sonnet. Gemini and Claude are more concise. — [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Antislop: slop fingerprints "cluster strongly within model families, but differ substantially between" them. — [arXiv 2510.15061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061)

**Ineffective indicators and signs of human writing (false-positive guidance)**
- WP lists features "more common in Wikipedia articles written by humans than in AI-generated text": simple is/has phrases ("there is a", "it has a"); plain verbs (wrote, moved, used, tried, died); superlatives or definitive claims ("one of the best", "is the only", "was the first"); **qualifiers and intensifiers ("very", "perhaps", "tends to")**; and **wordy constructions ("as a result of", "in order to", "all of the", "a part of", "the fact that")**. — [WP §Signs of human writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Ineffective as AI signs: perfect grammar, mixed registers, "bland" prose, "fancy" or "academic" prose in general, and **transition words in isolation** ("Additionally", "Consequently", "Notably"). WP: "only a few transition words … are known to be overused by AI … this is not a strong tell". — [WP §Ineffective indicators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- Reinhart: LLMs use agentless passives at about half the human rate. — [arXiv 2410.16107](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107)

### Inferences
- **participial-tail rule [NEW, top priority]**: a dependency pattern where token.tag_ == "VBG", token.dep_ ∈ {advcl, acl}, the head is the main verb or the object of an earlier clause, the clause is preceded by a comma, and it sits at the end of the sentence. Raise confidence when the VBG lemma ∈ {highlight, underscore, emphasize, reflect, symbolize, showcase, demonstrate, illustrate, contribute, foster, cultivate, enhance, ensure, solidify, cement, mark, signal, shape, pave, set (the stage), create, offer, provide, make, embody, reinforce} and the clause object contains a significance noun (importance, significance, role, legacy, relevance, commitment, influence, impact, heritage, value). Bare VBG tails ("He left, slamming the door") are common in human narrative, so require the lemma list or the significance-object condition. Expected false-positive risk is low to moderate.
- **"that"-subject clauses [NEW, low severity]**: "That X is Y suggests…" (csubj with "that" mark). Rare in human prose per Reinhart. Low frequency, so it is a weak but cheap signal.
- **negative parallelism [EXTEND]**: add (a) cross-sentence "isn't/is not X. (It's|It is|Rather,|Instead,) Y."; (b) em-dash "isn't (just )?X—it's Y"; (c) "no X, no Y, just Z" / "Not X, not Y, not Z — just W"; (d) "It's not about X; it's about Y"; (e) "X doesn't just V1; it V2". The Antislop Appendix L regexes are a ready-made, false-positive-tuned starting point. In spaCy, detect a negated copula or verb (dep_ == "neg") in sentence n and a sentence-initial "It's/It is/They're/Rather/Instead" with a parallel predicate in n+1. False-positive risk is moderate: human corrective statements ("It's not a bug, it's a feature" as an idiom) and legitimate contrast are common. Flag by density per document.
- **colon reveal [EXTEND question-then-answer]**: `^(The (result|answer|catch|kicker|irony|problem|truth|twist|takeaway|bottom line|upshot|reality|secret))\?` or the same phrase followed by `:` and a short fragment. Precision is high.
- **false range [NEW]**: "from X to Y" where X and Y are NOUN phrases that are not numbers, dates or places on a scale, especially paired possessives ("from its X to its Y") or "From X to Y, Z…" at the start of a sentence. The false-positive risk is real (literal ranges such as "from 2010 to 2020" or "from London to Paris"), so exclude NUM, DATE and GPE entities via spaCy NER.
- **elegant variation [NEW, hard]**: deterministic detection is approximate. One option: within a paragraph, find noun chunks that share a head-noun lemma or a WordNet synonym set yet vary their modifiers more than k times. It is hard to make precise, and WP flags the non-native false-positive risk. Treat it as an advisory, not an error.
- **challenge sandwich**: see section 2.
- **typography rules [NEW]**: (a) curly/straight quote inconsistency within one document, which is high-precision for pasted LLM text and also a plain consistency rule; (b) title-case headings in Markdown (`^#+ ` lines where more than 70% of non-stopwords are capitalised); (c) a bold-lead bullet ratio (`^\s*[-*] \*\*[^*]+\*\*:?` across more than 60% of bullets in a list); (d) emoji at the start of a heading or bullet; (e) literal `**` in non-markdown output; (f) spaced em-dash " — ". The existing em-dash rule could add a spaced-dash or density threshold. Given the Economist and WP findings, it should be demoted or tagged as "Claude-era/pre-Sept-2026" rather than treated as a universal tell.
- **punctuation sparsity [NEW, document-level metric]**: a low comma, semicolon and parenthesis rate per sentence combined with low sentence-length variance (coefficient of variation) and a high share of "and" coordinations. This fits as a document-level "uniform rhythm" advisory, not a per-sentence lint.
- **Conflict with existing rules**: WP's "signs of human writing" list puts intensifiers/qualifiers ("very", "perhaps", "tends to"), wordy constructions ("in order to", "the fact that"), expletives ("there is a") and plain is/has phrases on the *human* side. Clarity's intensifier, hedge, expletive and wordiness rules are good plain-English rules, but they do not mark AI text. Any "AI-likelihood" scoring or messaging should not count them as AI evidence. Likewise, Reinhart finds LLMs underuse agentless passives, so the passive rule is not an AI signal either. Transition-word rules (Additionally, Moreover, Notably) are only weak AI signals in isolation (WP), although sentence-initial "Additionally" is on the corroborated vocabulary list.

### Gaps
- No quantitative human-vs-LLM rates were found for colon reveals, false ranges, title-case headings, bold-lead bullets, emoji bullets or semicolon/colon overuse. These rest on Wikipedia editor observation and practitioner lists.
- The Economist article's exact numbers (em-dash rates per model, comma rates, sentence-length means) were reachable only through secondary summaries without figures. The primary article was not accessed.
- The Antislop Figure 7 per-model rates for "not x, but y" (per 1k characters) were not extractable from the PDF text. Only the "6.3× in some models" headline is available.
- Reinhart et al.'s full per-feature tables for the Llama variants were not read in detail.

## 4. Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing": coverage checklist (November 2026 revision)

### Takeaway
The page is organised into Content, Language and grammar, Style, Communication intended for the user, Markup, Citations, Comment-specific, Edit summaries, Miscellaneous, Signs of human writing, Ineffective indicators, and Historical indicators. It explicitly marks several tells as historical: promotional language (2023–2025), knowledge cutoffs (2022–2023), section summaries, prompt refusal, em-dashes (2022–September 2026), abrupt cut-offs, outdated access-dates and elegant variation. It also adds new 2026 tells: AIIDENTIFY "sources identify him as" (2026), corrective-framing disclaimers (mid-2026), "Y rather than X", and plain-word GPT-6 vocabulary.

### Cited Findings
All from [WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) and [WP/Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing/Style). The prose-relevant sections and their shortcuts:
- Content: Undue emphasis on significance/legacy (WP:AILEGACY) with the "influenced discussions" sub-sign; notability/attribution/media coverage (WP:AIATTR); identification by primary/tertiary sources (WP:AIIDENTIFY, 2026); online presence; superficial analyses (WP:SUPERFICIAL, -ing tails); vague connection or association (WP:AICONNECT); vague attributions and overgeneralisation (WP:AIWEASEL); outline-like challenges and future-prospects conclusions (WP:FACESCHALLENGES); "Awards and recognition" sections.
- Language and grammar: AI vocabulary (WP:AIWORDS, with era list); avoidance of basic copulatives (WP:AINOCOPULA); negative parallelisms (WP:AIPARALLEL) in three forms ("Not just X, but also Y"; "Not X, but Y" / "no…, no…, just…"; "Y rather than X"); leads treating lists or broad titles as proper nouns ("X refers to"); rule of three (WP:RO3).
- Style: title case, boldface overuse, inline-header lists, heading-level errors, emoji, curly quotes.
- Communication intended for the user: collaborative communication (WP:CERTAINLY); hedging disclaimers about source availability (WP:AIDISCLAIMER) in 2023–24, 2024–25 and mid-2026 forms; phrasal templates and placeholders (WP:AIPLACEHOLDER), including date and URL placeholders.
- Signs of human writing: simple is/has; plain verbs; superlatives; qualifiers and intensifiers; wordy constructions.
- Ineffective indicators: perfect grammar; mixed register; bland, fancy or formal prose; isolated transitions; unsourced content; odd wikitext.
- Historical: promotional language (WP:AIPUFFERY); knowledge cutoffs (WP:AICUTOFF); section summaries (WP:CONCLUSION); prompt refusal; em-dashes (WP:AIDASH); abrupt cut-offs; outdated access-dates; lexical diversity/elegant variation (WP:AIELEVAR).
- The page's caveat: "Detecting LLM texts on the basis of style alone is not as easy as it seems". GPT-4o (ChatGPT's model May 2024–August 2025) deviates from human writing more than its contemporaries.

### Inferences
- Signs the clarity linter can implement deterministically, ordered by rough precision: placeholders and assistant leakage > participial significance tails > challenge sandwich > copula avoidance with a significance object > negative-parallelism variants > notability and "identifies X as" boilerplate > corrective-framing disclaimers > vocabulary density > typography (title case, bold leads, curly quotes) > elegant variation.
- Wikipedia-specific signs (citations, DOIs, utm_source, AfC notes, edit summaries) are out of scope for a general prose linter.

### Gaps
- The page sections on markup irregularities, citations and edit summaries were skimmed via headings only, because they are not prose-level.

## 5. Dead physical metaphors applied to abstractions (Claude Opus 4.8/5 and GPT-5/6 era), plus newest salience tics

### Takeaway
This family is real and well documented in 2026 practitioner sources, but almost none of it is measured. The single exception is "load-bearing", which is the flagship "Claudism": 3 uses in about 53k words of Opus 5 blog output in Arize's eval, and named in every Claudish write-up. The best catalogue is the community banlist **claudisms.ai**, which groups these verbs as "pseudo-weighty placement/transport/motion verbs applied to abstractions": *hold, carry, lives, moves, points, lands, rides along, hands, turns on, sit with, surface, shape, the engine, physics, compounds, earns its keep, doing the work / heavy lifting, unpack, lean into, reaching for, settled, quietly*. Wikipedia's mid-2026 (GPT-6) vocabulary independently lists *echoed, steady, quietly, matters, clearer, dependable*. Neither Wikipedia nor the antislop fiction lists mention "holds", "lands", "tracks", "sits", "maps to" or "checks out". Those come from user observation and the claudisms banlist, so the evidence for them is anecdotal. The closest measured neighbours are salience phrases: "this matters" at 116× and "why X matters" at 92× the human rate for Claude Opus 5.5, from Graphite (October 2026), and "This matters." at 1.67 per 1k words in Opus 5 vs 0.93 in Opus 5.5 (Arize).

### Inferences
Detection design for clarity. Every rule below is a dependency-plus-concreteness check: flag when the relevant argument noun's Brysbaert concreteness is **below about 3.0** (on the 1–5 scale; tune it), or when the argument is a pronoun ("it", "this", "that") whose antecedent is not resolvable as concrete. Pronoun subjects are the main hard case. Treat demonstrative "this/that" subjects as abstract by default, because "That tracks." and "This lands." are almost always metaphorical. Shapes per item:

| Item | Metaphorical shape (flag) | Literal shape (skip) |
|---|---|---|
| **hold** | intransitive (no dobj) with an abstract nsubj ("the plan/argument/logic/claim holds", "holds up", "holds true"); or dobj ∈ {tension, line, thought, space, idea, contradiction} ("hold the tension", "hold space") | dobj concrete ("holds the shelf"); "hold" + prt "on"; nsubj human + concrete dobj |
| **land** | intransitive with an abstract nsubj (argument, joke, point, message, change, feature, PR: "the argument lands", "the point landed"); "lands hardest"; "where I landed" (human nsubj + "where" clause, no physical location); transitive "land the change/PR" | nsubj ∈ vehicles/animals/objects (plane, bird, ball); prep "on/at" + GPE/FAC |
| **track** | intransitive with nsubj ∈ {that, this, it, number, figure, story, data}, with no dobj and no prep "with" ("That tracks.", "the number tracks") | transitive with dobj ("track the package"); nsubj human + dobj |
| **sit** | nsubj abstract + prep "in/at/on/between/inside" + abstract pobj ("where this sits in the roadmap", "risk sits with the vendor"); "sit with" + abstract pobj | nsubj animate + concrete location ("sat on the bench") |
| **live** | nsubj abstract + prep "in/at" ("the logic lives in the handler", "the risk lives in the gap"). Note: in software docs "config lives in /etc" is accepted usage, so give it low severity | nsubj animate |
| **surface** (verb) | VERB with dobj abstract (insight, issue, pattern, signal, risk) | NOUN uses; dobj concrete ("surface the road"); intransitive with a submarine/diver nsubj |
| **map** | "map to/onto" with both nsubj and pobj abstract ("this maps to our goals"); intransitive with no dobj | technical: dobj or pobj is a key/field/column/address/port. Code contexts are very common here, so suppress inside code spans and in docs that mention data structures |
| **check out** | nsubj ∈ {math, numbers, logic, story, claim, it} + prt "out", intransitive ("the math checks out") | transitive with a human nsubj ("check out the book"); "checked out" of a hotel |
| **stand** | intransitive with nsubj ∈ {point, argument, claim, criticism, result, rule} ("the point stands") | animate nsubj; "stand + prep" location |
| **earn** | "earns its (place/keep/spot/seat/slot/counter space)" with an inanimate nsubj | animate nsubj + money dobj |
| **do work / heavy lifting** | nsubj inanimate or linguistic (word, sentence, clause, assumption, "just", "really") + dobj lemma "work" or "lifting", plus intensifier "a lot of / most of / all the / heavy" | animate nsubj |
| **load-bearing** | ADJ (or compound) modifying an abstract noun (assumption, sentence, claim, test, detail), or predicative with an abstract nsubj | modifies wall/beam/column/structure. Essentially always flag outside architecture and engineering text |
| **carry** | dobj ∈ {weight, cost, risk, argument, meaning} with an inanimate nsubj ("the sentence carries the weight") | animate nsubj + concrete dobj; finance "carry a balance" (domain exception) |
| **unpack** | dobj abstract (claim, idea, this, what…) or intransitive "let's unpack" | dobj ∈ {box, bag, suitcase, archive, tuple} (common in Python docs, so suppress in code contexts) |
| **anchor** | VERB with dobj abstract ("anchor the discussion", "anchors the strategy"); passive "anchored in values" | nautical or physical dobj; "anchor" in HTML/links |
| **lean into** | phrasal ("lean" + prep "into") + abstract pobj ("lean into the discomfort") | pobj concrete ("leaned into the wind"), animate subject |
| **double down** | always idiomatic. Human cliché, so low severity at most | none |
| **move the needle** | fixed idiom. Human business cliché, so low severity | literal gauges (rare) |
| **zoom out** | intransitive, human nsubj or imperative, with no camera or map context ("Let's zoom out") | dobj or context includes map/image/view/camera/UI |
| **point** (intr.) | "points to/toward" + abstract pobj with an abstract nsubj ("the evidence points toward") | animate nsubj + concrete pobj; note "points to" is common in human analytical prose, so low severity |
| **turn on** | "turns on" + abstract pobj = "depends on" | dobj device ("turn on the light"); prt reading |
| **ride on / ride along** | inanimate nsubj | animate nsubj + vehicle |
| **hand back / hands you** | inanimate nsubj (model, tool, API) + dobj abstract | animate nsubj + concrete dobj |

- **Which items have the strongest evidence?** load-bearing; doing (a lot of) the work / heavy lifting; earns its keep/place; hold (abstract); lives (abstract placement); lands; surface (verb); unpack; lean into; carry (abstract). All are on claudisms.ai, and the first three are also in the Arize data. "Tracks", "sits", "maps to", "checks out", "the point stands" and "anchor" are plausible extrapolations, attested only by the user's own observation. Ship them at lower severity, or as one "dead physical metaphor" rule whose message cites the family rather than individual evidence.
- **Severity by density**: Arize found phrase lists catch only 9 of 153 human-flagged Claudisms, and the lexicon shifted between Opus 5 and 5.5. So a single per-sentence hit should be advisory. Two or more family hits per about 300 words is a much stronger signal.
- **False positives**: technical writing legitimately uses "lives in" (file locations), "maps to" (data mapping), "surfaces" (UI surfaces an error), "holds" (a variable holds a value; note a variable or register is abstract and will pass a concreteness filter, so add a whitelist of programming nsubjs/dobjs: variable, field, key, value, column, buffer, register, pointer, list, array, map, dict, object, function, config, file). Analytical prose uses "points to", "holds true" and "the argument holds" (philosophy and logic) at decent human rates, so keep these low severity. Brysbaert also rates some abstract-use nouns fairly high (e.g. "point" about 3.5, "line" about 4), so add a sense list for nouns that are concrete in form but used abstractly (point, line, weight, space, picture, shape, engine, thread, lens, seam, needle).
- **Salience companions to add alongside** [NEW]: "This matters." / "(and) that matters" / "why X matters" / "what (actually|really) matters" / "the X matters more"; "genuinely"; "honest(ly)" as a self-qualifier ("the honest version", "honest take"); "the trap is"; "the tell (is)"; "the whole point/game"; "worth stating plainly"; "full stop" sentence-final; "key insight"; "deserves a moment". Graphite measured "this matters" at 116× and "why X matters" at 92× (Claude Opus 5.5). "Dependable" ≥23× (Opus 5.5) is also on WP's GPT-6 list.
