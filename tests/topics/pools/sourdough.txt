# Baking sourdough bread at home

Sourdough bread starts with a starter, a mix of flour and water that holds wild yeast and bacteria. Feed the starter with fresh flour and water every day until it doubles a few hours after each feeding.

A healthy starter smells sour and a little sweet, and it rises and falls on a steady schedule. Keep the starter warm, feed it the same flour each time, and use it for bread when it peaks after a feeding.

Mix the dough from bread flour, water, salt and a spoon of active starter. Stir the flour and water first and let the dough rest for an hour so the flour absorbs the water before you add the salt.

Strong bread flour gives the dough more gluten, and gluten traps the gas the starter makes. A dough made with weak flour spreads flat in the oven, while a strong flour dough holds its shape and rises tall.

During the first rise, fold the dough every half hour. Wet your hands, lift one side of the dough, stretch it up and fold it over. Four sets of folds build the gluten without any hard kneading.

The first rise of sourdough takes four to eight hours, depending on the warmth of the kitchen and the strength of the starter. The dough is ready when it has grown by half and shows bubbles on the sides.

Shape the dough into a tight round loaf on a lightly floured counter. Pull the dough toward you so the surface tightens, then set the loaf seam side up in a floured basket for the second rise.

Most bakers give the shaped loaf a long cold rise in the fridge overnight. The cold slows the yeast, deepens the sour flavor of the bread, and makes the dough firmer and easier to score.

Bake sourdough in a heavy covered pot heated in a very hot oven. The lid traps steam from the dough, the crust stays soft for the first part of the bake, and the loaf rises fully before the crust sets.

Score the top of the loaf with a sharp blade just before it goes into the hot pot. A deep score lets the bread open along a chosen line instead of tearing on the side where the crust is weakest.

Remove the lid after twenty minutes and bake the bread until the crust is dark brown. A dark crust tastes better than a pale one, and the loaf should sound hollow when you tap the bottom.

Let the baked loaf cool on a rack for at least an hour before you cut it. The crumb is still setting as the bread cools, and a loaf cut too early tastes gummy and tears under the knife.
