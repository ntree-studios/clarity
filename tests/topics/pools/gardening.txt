# Growing tomatoes in a vegetable garden

Tomatoes need a sunny spot in the vegetable garden with at least eight hours of direct sun. Plants grown in shade stay thin and pale, flower late, and set far fewer tomatoes by the end of the summer.

Start tomato seeds indoors six weeks before the last frost. Sow the seeds in small pots of moist seed compost, keep the pots warm, and move the seedlings to bright light as soon as the first leaves open.

Harden off the tomato seedlings for a week before planting them in the garden. Set the pots outside for a few hours each day, then for longer, so the young plants get used to sun, wind and cool nights.

Plant tomatoes deeper than they grew in their pots. Roots form along the buried part of the stem, and a tomato plant with a deep root system finds more water in the garden soil during dry weeks.

Tomatoes grow best in rich, loose garden soil with plenty of compost. Dig compost into the bed before planting, and add a little more compost around each plant in the middle of the summer.

Water tomato plants deeply at the base two or three times a week rather than a little every day. Deep watering sends the roots down into the soil, and dry leaves keep the plants free of leaf disease.

A layer of straw mulch around the tomato plants keeps the soil moist and cool. The mulch also stops soil splashing onto the lower leaves when you water, which is how many tomato diseases spread.

Tall tomato varieties need a stake or a cage to hold the plants upright. Tie the main stem to the stake every week as the plants grow, using soft twine that will not cut into the stem.

Pinch out the side shoots that grow between the main stem and the leaves of tall tomato plants. Removing these shoots sends more of the plant's energy into fewer, larger tomatoes on the main stem.

Feed tomato plants every two weeks once the first flowers open. A feed high in potassium helps the plants set fruit, while too much nitrogen gives large green plants with few tomatoes.

Pick tomatoes when they are fully colored and slightly soft at the stem end. Tomatoes left too long on the plant split after heavy rain, and fruit picked green never reaches the full flavor of a ripe one.

At the end of the season, pull up the old tomato plants and add them to the compost heap unless they show disease. Grow the next tomatoes in a different bed of the garden to keep soil diseases from building up.
