# How to pull a good espresso shot

A good espresso shot starts with fresh coffee beans and a burr grinder. Grind the coffee fine, close to the texture of table salt, so the water meets resistance in the basket and the shot runs slowly.

Weigh the dose of ground coffee before every shot. Most baskets hold eighteen grams, and a scale removes the guesswork. The same dose and the same grind give the same espresso from one morning to the next.

Distribute the ground coffee evenly in the basket and tamp it level. An uneven puck lets water channel through the weak side, and the espresso shot tastes sour and thin because the grind was not extracted evenly.

Water temperature matters as much as the grind. Most espresso machines heat water to about ninety three degrees. Cooler water pulls a sour shot, and hotter water pulls a bitter shot from the same coffee dose.

Time the shot from the moment the pump starts. A balanced espresso shot runs for about twenty eight seconds and yields about twice the dose in liquid coffee. Adjust the grind finer when the shot runs fast.

Taste every espresso shot and change one thing at a time. If the shot is sour, grind finer or raise the water temperature. If the shot is bitter, grind coarser or shorten the time the water runs through the coffee.

Fresh beans give a better espresso than old beans. Buy coffee roasted within the last month, store it sealed and away from light, and grind it right before the shot so the dose keeps its aroma and crema.

The crema on top of the espresso shot tells you about the grind and the dose. Thin pale crema means the coffee was ground too coarse or the beans are stale. Dark spotted crema means the water and grind are right.

Back to the espresso. Once the grind and dose are right, the next step is milk for a latte. Steam the milk with the wand just below the surface until it is warm and glossy, then pour it into the espresso shot.

Cleaning the espresso machine keeps every shot tasting fresh. Flush water through the group head after each shot, wipe the steam wand, and backflush the machine each week so old coffee oils never reach the next dose.

Descale the espresso machine every few months if your water is hard. Scale builds up in the boiler, lowers the water temperature, and the shot pulls sour even when the grind and the coffee dose are right.

A good grinder matters more than an expensive espresso machine. A burr grinder gives an even grind for every dose, and an even grind lets the water extract the coffee evenly so each shot tastes balanced and sweet.
