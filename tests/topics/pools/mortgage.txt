# Choosing a home loan

Buying a home usually means taking out a mortgage loan from a bank. The bank looks at your credit score, your income, and your debt before it decides how much it will lend and at what interest rate.

The interest rate on a mortgage loan sets most of the monthly payment. A fixed rate keeps the payment the same for the life of the loan, while an adjustable rate can rise when the bank changes its rates.

A larger down payment lowers the loan amount and the monthly mortgage payment. Many lenders ask for twenty percent down, and a smaller down payment often adds mortgage insurance to every monthly payment on the home.

Your credit score affects the interest rate a bank will offer on a home loan. A higher credit score usually earns a lower rate, and a lower rate saves thousands over the life of a thirty year mortgage.

Closing costs add to the price of a home loan. The bank charges fees for the appraisal, the credit report and the title search, and these costs are paid at closing along with the first mortgage payment.

Refinancing replaces an old mortgage loan with a new one at a lower interest rate. Homeowners refinance when rates fall, and a lower rate can cut the monthly payment or shorten the term of the loan.

Paying extra toward the principal of a mortgage loan shortens the loan and lowers the total interest. Even a small extra payment each month reduces the balance faster and saves money on interest over the years.

Before signing a mortgage, compare loan offers from more than one bank. Look at the interest rate, the closing costs, and the monthly payment, and check how your credit score changes the rate each lender offers.
