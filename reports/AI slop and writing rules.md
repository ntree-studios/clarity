# Structure, Not Vocabulary, Now Exposes Machine Prose

LLM prose fails in a layered, predictable way. At the word level it overuses a style lexicon whose composition changes with each model generation. At the sentence level it attaches participial "significance" tails, contrastive "not X, it's Y" frames and copula substitutes. At the paragraph level it repeats one shape, links sentences with additive glue instead of causal logic, and ends on a statement of importance. At the document level it pads, generalises, drifts from the heading and declines to reach a verdict. The best-measured signals are structural, not lexical. GPT-4o writes present participial clauses at **5.3× the human rate** ([Reinhart et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107)), and some models produce "It's not X, it's Y" **6.3×** as often as people do ([Antislop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061)). By contrast, a phrase list caught only **9 of 153** human-flagged Claudisms in one 2026 evaluation ([Arize](https://arize.com/blog/anthropic-says-it-fixed-claudes-writing/)). The 2026 tic of putting physical verbs on abstract subjects ("the plan holds", "the argument lands", "load-bearing assumption") is widely catalogued but almost entirely unmeasured. Good writing technique points the opposite way from slop: one point per paragraph, a stated topic, old information before new, and global coherence rather than raw connective counts. Comprehension research supports global paragraph-to-paragraph overlap as a quality signal and finds local cohesion counts useless or negative ([McNamara, Crossley & McCarthy 2010](https://glotta.ntua.gr/NLP/NLP-PostGrad/2018/State-Of-The-Art-Creative_Writing/Linguistic_Features_of_Writing_Quality.pdf)). For clarity, the highest-value additions are a dependency rule for participial tails, a concreteness-gated rule for abstract physical verbs, artifact rules for assistant leakage and placeholders, and composite and positional rules for significance endings, recap closers, generic paragraphs and heading drift. These should come with section-aware genre profiles, because the right answer on passives, hedges and bullets flips between business, science and technical writing. Two framing changes matter as much as the new rules. Clarity's hedge, passive, intensifier and expletive rules mark weak prose, not machine prose. And every AI lexicon needs era tags and periodic refreshes.

## Slop stacks from word to document, and its lexicon expires each model generation

### Word level: a style lexicon of "showing" verbs that drifts by era

The excess-vocabulary studies agree on what the lexicon looks like. It is mostly verbs and participles of showing and emphasising, plus a few evaluative adjectives and abstract nouns. Across more than 15 million PubMed abstracts, Kobak et al. found **"delves" at 28.0×** its counterfactual 2024 frequency, "underscores" at 13.8× and "showcasing" at 10.7×. **66% of the 379 excess style words were verbs** and 14% adjectives. At least **13.5% of 2024 abstracts** were LLM-processed ([Kobak et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016)). Their public list of 900 excess words includes the long tail clarity does not yet cover: *bolster, encapsulate, elucidate, garner, harness, illuminate, interplay, intricate, meticulous, multifaceted, nuanced, pivotal, realm, seamless, showcase, streamline, underscore, unveil* ([berenslab/llm-excess-vocab](https://github.com/berenslab/llm-excess-vocab)). In peer reviews, "meticulous" rose **34.7×**, "intricate" 11.2× and "commendable" 9.8× ([Liang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07183v4)). In GPT-4o creative and essay output, "camaraderie" ran at 162× and "tapestry" at 155× the human rate. Gaps were larger for instruction-tuned models than for base models ([Reinhart et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107)).

The lexicon has a shelf life, and that is the main design constraint. Wikipedia's editors track it by era. GPT-4 favoured *delve, tapestry, testament*. GPT-4o favoured *align with, fostering, showcasing*. GPT-5 leaned on -ing participles (*emphasizing, enhancing, highlighting*). Mid-2026 output reportedly prefers plain words such as *clearer, dependable, steady, practical, quietly, matters* ([Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). "Delve" dropped off sharply in 2025. Graphite measured Claude Opus 5.5 using "this matters" at **116×** and "why X matters" at **92×** the human rate, and "dependable" at 23× or more ([Mezha summary of Graphite](https://mezha.ua/en/news/researchers-find-ai-writing-tells-315774/)). Antislop shows the fingerprints "cluster strongly within model families" ([Paech et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061)). A static list therefore decays. Density also matters more than presence: Wikipedia says one or two such words "may be coincidental", but many words, many times, is "one of the strongest tells". Fiction has its own lexicon. "Flickered" appears in the over-represented list of **98.5% of 67 models**, and "voice barely above a whisper" in 68.7% ([Antislop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061)). That list is useless for expository prose.

### Sentence level: participial tails, contrast frames and copula avoidance

The present participial tail ("…, highlighting the importance of X") is the best-quantified sentence-shape tic. GPT-4o uses it at **5.3× the human rate (Cohen's d = 1.38)** and instruction-tuned Llama at 2–5×. The same study measured "that"-clauses as subject at 2.6×, nominalizations at 2.1× and phrasal coordination at 1.9×. Agentless passives ran at only **about 0.5×** ([Reinhart et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107)). Wikipedia's "superficial analysis" examples show the shape: "…stood at approximately 56,998 inhabitants, *creating a lively community within its borders*" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)).

Contrastive framing is the tic people notice most. OpenAI calls it "contrastive phrasing" and calls it the hardest to train out ([AI Weekly on The Atlantic](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/atlantic-ais-its-not-x-its-y-tic-proves-hardest-to-shake)). It now appears in forms that clarity's existing `escalation-not-just` and `x-rather-than-y` rules miss. The cross-sentence form is "This dispersal is not dissolution. Rather, it constitutes…". The em-dash form is "isn't just sourcing—it's framing". The reductive form is "no X, no Y, just Z" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). Antislop publishes false-positive-tuned regexes for this family in its Appendix L ([Paech et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061)). Copula avoidance is quieter but measurable. One study found an **over 10% decrease in "is" and "are"** in academic writing in 2023, as "serves as", "stands as", "boasts" and "features" took their place ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). Colon reveals ("The result? A cleaner architecture."), false ranges ("from its scenic landscapes to its historical landmarks"), synonym cycling and challenge sandwiches ("Despite these challenges, X continues to thrive") are documented by editors and practitioners but have no measured rates ([tessl anti-patterns](https://tessl.io/registry/jbaruch/blog-writer/1.1.0/files/references/ai-anti-patterns.md); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)).

Typography has reversed. Em-dashes were a strong tell through September 2026, but the Economist's July 2026 study found **only Claude still exceeds human em-dash rates**. LLMs now use *fewer* commas, semicolons and parentheses than humans, write long sentences with little variation, and use "and" more than any other word ([Daring Fireball on The Economist](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/08/11/economist-ai-writing)). Arize measured Opus 5 at 12.9 em-dashes per 1,000 words and Opus 5.5 at 0.05 ([Arize](https://arize.com/blog/anthropic-says-it-fixed-claudes-writing/)). The em-dash rule should stay as a style preference, but it no longer works as a general AI signal.

### Paragraph level: one repeated shape, additive glue and a significance ending

The paragraph-level evidence comes from register and cohesion research, not tic lists. Coh-Metrix comparisons found ChatGPT strong on **referential cohesion** (repeated nouns) and weak on **deep cohesion** (causal and intentional links) ([Zhou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11812)). ChatGPT essays lean on interactive metadiscourse, meaning transitions and "as discussed above" markers. They use fewer hedges, boosters and attitude markers, so they read as impersonal ([Jiang & Hyland](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/99123)). The result is surface glue ("Additionally", "Furthermore", a restated noun) over shallow logic. The typical paragraph runs claim, explanation, significance, with the last sentence carrying a legacy word or participial tail. Wikipedia calls this "undue emphasis on significance and legacy" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). AI-assisted student essays lost **70–78% of the variance in their cohesion architecture** ([Inoshita et al., preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21228)), which fits a paragraph template stamped repeatedly. A related failure is the bare demonstrative. In "Microservices can be deployed independently, scaled per service, and written in different languages. This makes them powerful", "This" points at a whole list. No study counts these in LLM versus human text, so the bare-demonstrative signal is an inference.

### Document level: padding, genericity, drift and no verdict

The document-level picture comes from copy-editors. Shaib et al. had three professional editors annotate 250 texts and found about **34–35% judged slop**. **Relevance, Density and Tone** were the strongest predictors (β ≈ 0.06, 0.05, 0.05). Simple automatic proxies did **not** predict the verdict on their own: word count, compression ratio, templates per token and Gunning Fog. LLM judges agreed with humans at κ ≈ 0 ([Shaib et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19163v2)). That finding sets a design constraint. Padding rules must be positional (opener, closer, last sentence of each paragraph) and relative (information compared with the rest of the document), not raw length limits.

The training incentives explain the failures. Length dominates human preference votes, with a coefficient of 0.249 against 0.024 for headers ([LMSYS](https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-08-28-style-control)). Reward models prefer list-formatted answers **89.5%** of the time and over-reward "Fluff", "Fog" (vagueness) and "Flattery" ([Bharadwaj et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05339v2)). GPT-4-Turbo judges prefer bolded responses 89.5% of the time ([From Lists to Emojis](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11704v2)). The outputs follow: preambles, recap closers, bold lead-in bullets, title-case headings, "Key Takeaways" appendages, and "it depends on your specific needs" conclusions. Genericity is real but subtle. Instruction-tuned models keep a noun-heavy, nominalized style even when asked to write informally ([Reinhart et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v2)). Yet LLM news continuations use *more* numbers than human news ([Muñoz-Ortiz et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09067v3)). An absolute "no numerals" rule would therefore misfire. A within-document "zero-anchor paragraph" rule works better. Assistant leakage ("I hope this helps", "Certainly!", "[Your Name]", "INSERT_SOURCE_URL") remains the only near-certain artifact class ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)).

The negative evidence matters as much. Wikipedia lists hedges and intensifiers ("very", "perhaps", "tends to"), wordy constructions ("in order to", "the fact that"), plain is/has sentences and "there is" as features **more common in human writing**. Isolated transitions are listed as ineffective indicators ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). LLMs under-use agentless passives. Jiang & Hyland find ChatGPT uses *fewer* hedges than students. Clarity's hedge, passive, intensifier, expletive and wordiness rules are sound plain-English rules, but any "AI-likelihood" messaging must not count them as evidence of machine authorship.

### Physical verbs on abstract subjects: widely catalogued, almost unmeasured

The family the question names is real, but its evidence is the thinnest of any category here. The community banlist claudisms.ai groups these as "pseudo-weighty placement/transport/motion verbs applied to abstractions". Its entries include *hold* ("the line it holds"), *carry* ("the sentence carries the weight"), *lives* ("the risk lives in the gap"), *lands*, *points*, *turns on*, *rides on*, *hands back*, *surface*, *unpack*, *lean into*, *sit with*, *compounds*, *doing the heavy lifting*, *earns its keep* and *load-bearing* ([claudisms.ai](https://claudisms.ai/claudisms.md)). "Load-bearing" is the flagship tic, said to originate with Opus 4.8 ([explainx](https://explainx.ai/blog/claude-opus-5-load-bearing-claudisms-writing-tells-2026)). It was the subject of a widely discussed hook that rewrites it ([jola.dev](https://jola.dev/posts/how-to-stop-claude-from-saying-load-bearing)). Arize counted it 3 times in about 53,000 words of Opus 5 output, and found "earns its keep/place" and "is doing a lot of work" still present in Opus 5.5. In that evaluation, stock metaphors made up only about **10 of 153** human flags. Salience frames ("This matters.", 1.67 per 1,000 words in Opus 5) and contrast reframes made up most of the rest ([Arize](https://arize.com/blog/anthropic-says-it-fixed-claudes-writing/)).

No source found any corpus frequency for "the plan holds", "that tracks", "where this sits", "this maps to", "the math checks out" or "the point stands". These are plausible siblings of the catalogued verbs, but the evidence for them is user observation. Several are also normal human English in some registers. Philosophers write "the argument holds". Software docs write "config lives in /etc", "the key maps to a column" and "the variable holds a value". Analysts write "the evidence points to". The defensible rule is therefore narrow. Flag the metaphorical *shape* (intransitive use or an abstract argument, gated by noun concreteness), whitelist programming and data nouns, keep single hits at low severity, and raise severity on density. The rule design appears in the rules section below.

## Sound technique: one point per paragraph, old before new, global over local cohesion

The writing authorities agree on paragraph construction, and their agreement maps onto checkable features. Strunk's rule is "one paragraph to each topic". Begin "with a topic sentence; end it in conformity with the beginning", and avoid "ending with a digression, or with an unimportant detail" ([Strunk](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/37134/37134-h/37134-h.htm)). Purdue lists unity, coherence, a topic sentence and adequate development, and warns against starting with one point and drifting into another ([Purdue OWL](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/paragraphs_and_paragraphing/index.html)). Williams says a reader judges a paragraph coherent when she can find a sentence that states its point ([Writing Commons on Williams & Bizup](https://writingcommons.org/section/style/)). The guides disagree on length. GOV.UK caps paragraphs at **5 sentences** ([GOV.UK](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)), while Purdue warns against paragraphs of only two or three sentences. Strunk rejects single-sentence paragraphs except as transitions. All of this is opinion. No comprehension study on topic-sentence placement turned up.

Information flow inside the paragraph has the strongest theoretical grounding. Gopen & Swan give seven reader-expectation principles. Follow the subject quickly with its verb. Put old, backward-linking information in the topic position and emphasised new information in the stress position at the end. Put the action in the verb ([Gopen & Swan 1990](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/gopen_and_swan_science_of_scientific_writing.pdf)). They warn against reducing this to "old first, new last" and against word limits, defining a sentence as too long "when it has more viable candidates for stress positions than there are stress positions available". Williams adds characters as subjects, actions as verbs, and stable topic strings ([Style summary](https://sobrief-staging.onrender.com/books/style-2)). Daneš's constant and linear thematic progression and Centering Theory's preference for CONTINUE over ROUGH-SHIFT transitions formalise the same idea: keep the focal entity stable and link each sentence back to the last ([Kibble 2001](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix_video/J01-4007.pdf)). Barzilay and Lapata's entity grid tracks the same thing: each entity's grammatical role across sentences ([Barzilay & Lapata 2008](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix_video/J08-1001.pdf)).

The empirical record corrects a common linter assumption. In 120 rated college essays, **none of 26 Coh-Metrix cohesion indices** distinguished high- from low-quality essays. The best predictors were syntactic complexity, lexical diversity (MTLD) and word frequency, which together explained 22% of variance ([McNamara, Crossley & McCarthy 2010](https://glotta.ntua.gr/NLP/NLP-PostGrad/2018/State-Of-The-Art-Creative_Writing/Linguistic_Features_of_Writing_Quality.pdf)). Later work found local cohesion generally *negatively* associated with quality and **global, paragraph-to-paragraph overlap positively** associated ([eScholarship review](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5zv9m93j/qt5zv9m93j.pdf?t=sgibwg)). Connectives speed processing but did not improve recall in Sanders & Noordman. The type of coherence relation did matter: problem-solution beat list ([Tilburg abstract](https://repository.tilburguniversity.edu/items/35f9dadc-ca9c-4904-b962-3fcdd68cd1ba)). Pinker's advice is "the right number" of connectives. Too few leave readers puzzled, and too many impose connections that are not there ([Geoff Hart review](https://geoff-hart.com/articles/2016/sense-of-style.html)). For a linter, three implications follow. Flag *missing* links (no shared lemma, pronoun or connective) rather than rewarding more overlap. Flag additive connectives that bridge nothing. Measure heading-to-body and paragraph-to-paragraph overlap as the coherence signal that actually tracks quality. There is one tension. Essay raters reward more words before the main verb (r = .34), which contradicts Gopen & Swan's reader-ease advice. Clarity should say plainly that it optimises for reader ease.

Staying on subject is defined positively: one point per unit, a stated governing thought, answer first. Minto puts the governing thought on top with MECE supports ([untools](https://untools.co/minto-pyramid/)). BLUF puts "the recommendation, conclusion, or reason for writing in the first or second paragraph" ([JMU Army-style handout](https://www.jmu.edu/uwc/tutor-resources/MSL202L15%20Writing%20in%20the%20Army%20Style.pdf)). Microsoft says "Get to the point fast. Lead with what's most important" ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/top-10-tips-style-voice)). Google says "Don't pre-announce anything" ([Google style highlights](https://developers.google.com/style/highlights)). No guide defines digression operationally. Gopen & Swan's revision test, discarding "material for which we could not discern significant connections", is the closest, and it reduces to an overlap test against neighbours, topic sentence and heading. On concision, Orwell, Strunk, GOV.UK, Google and Microsoft converge on a familiar set: active voice, short common words, cut needless words, concrete language, "you", few nominalizations, no stale figures. The only hard number is GOV.UK's 25-word sentence limit. Orwell's first rule, "Never use a metaphor, simile or other figure of speech which you are used to seeing in print" ([Orwell](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/)), is the classical warrant for flagging dead physical metaphors regardless of who wrote them.

## Genre flips the right answer on passives, hedges and bullets

Genre changes the correct threshold more than any other variable, and the strongest quantitative findings are section-specific. Published academic prose is the most passive-heavy register, at **about 25% of finite verbs**, against about 2% in conversation ([Longman Student Grammar](https://vdoc.pub/documents/longman-student-grammar-of-spoken-and-written-english-4gkoo039ija0)). Nature still "prefer[s] authors to write in the active voice" ([Nature blog](https://blogs.nature.com/blog/active_is_better_than_passive/)), and Methods sections remain traditionally passive ([CASRAI](https://casrai.org/dictionary/term/active-voice-vs-passive-voice)). Hedging in science is legitimate epistemic marking, not slop. Hyland counted **20.6 hedges per 1,000 words** in biology articles, **36 per 1,000 in Discussions** and about 3.6 in Methods. **43%** of hedges shared a sentence with another hedge ([Hyland 1995](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED390258.pdf)). A broader eight-discipline corpus gave about 14.6 per 1,000 ([Hyland 2005](https://www.everettsd.org/cms/lib/WA01920133/Centricity/Domain/965/hyland.pdf)). In science the AI failure is excess vocabulary, unsupported boosters and uncited "studies show" claims, not hedge counts. LLM use is about twice as likely in Discussion paragraphs as in Methods ([Holzwarth et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10715)).

Business writing tightens where science relaxes. The SEC handbook lists long sentences, passive voice, weak verbs, superfluous words, jargon and abstract words as the common problems. It singles out "passive with agent deleted". It tells writers to find hidden -tion verbs, use "we" and "you", and allow "No multiple negatives" ([SEC Plain English Handbook](https://www.sec.gov/pdf/handbook.pdf)). Garner recommends an average of about **20 words per sentence** ([LawProse](https://lawprose.org/lawprose-lesson-269-average-sentence-length/)). Amazon's narrative memos replace slides with prose ([Bezos 2017 letter](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2017-letter-to-shareholders)). Secondary accounts of Amazon's rules add "replace adjectives with data" and "no weasel words" ([Lasn](https://www.trevorlasn.com/blog/amazons-no-weasel-words-rule)). On bullets the authorities disagree outright. The SEC says "Use bullets … wherever possible", while Amazon's narratives exist to force connected reasoning. A business profile should not penalise bullets by default. A narrative variant should.

Technical documentation converges on active voice, second person, present tense, numbered steps with conditions before instructions, and no "ease" words. Google flags "simple, simply, easy, easily, quick, quickly" because they "introduce an opinion that might not reflect the user's experience" ([Google inclusive docs](https://developers.google.com/style/inclusive-documentation)). It allows passive only to emphasise the object or when the actor does not matter ([Google voice](https://developers.google.com/style/voice)). Diátaxis splits docs into tutorials (learner-facing "we" allowed), how-to guides, neutral reference, and explanation, which "can contain opinions" ([Diátaxis](https://diataxis.fr/start-here/)). Against these sources, clarity's existing `technical` profile is partly unsourced. Loosening `construction` ×1.5 on the theory that "passive voice is normal in runbooks" contradicts Google and Microsoft. Disabling `long-sentence` contradicts Microsoft's "Shorter is always better". The real problem is code tokens inflating length, and the fix is to exclude code spans from the count. The `marketing` profile's hedges ×2.0 and grade 7–10 band also have no genre source. News sits between conversation and academic prose at about 15% passive and requires named attribution. Legal plain-language guidance shares business's 20-word average.

## Rules to add: positional, dependency-gated and density-scored

The rules below fit clarity's existing backends and condition vocabulary (`requires_all`, `suppress_if`, `active_when`, `allowance_when`). They follow a three-tier severity scheme that the 2025–26 Vale and textlint AI-slop packages converged on independently: artifact = error, likely tic = warning, common in human writing = info ([jdkato/voices](https://github.com/jdkato/voices); [vale-signs-of-ai-writing](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://ammil.industries/signs-of-ai-writing-a-vale-ruleset)). Those packages are almost entirely regex. A dependency-gated version should cut the false positives they accept, such as negative-parallel regexes firing on legitimate "not only … but also". No published false-positive rates exist for any AI-slop linter, so every threshold below is a starting point to be calibrated with clarity's `bootstrap` good/bad measurement.

| `fiction-slop` (opt-in, fiction profile) | "voice barely above a whisper", "took a deep breath", "couldn't help but feel", "air thick with the scent", flickered/murmured/gaze density | fiction only | info | [Antislop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061) |

### Dependency backend: participial tails, copula inflation and abstract physical verbs

`participial-tail` is the top-priority addition. It matches a token with `tag_ == "VBG"` and `dep_` in {advcl, acl}, preceded by a comma and ending the sentence. Confidence rises when the VBG lemma is in {highlight, underscore, emphasize, reflect, symbolize, showcase, contribute, foster, cultivate, enhance, ensure, solidify, cement, mark, signal, pave, embody, reinforce, create, provide} or its object heads a significance noun (importance, significance, role, legacy, relevance, impact, heritage). Bare narrative tails ("He left, slamming the door") stay unflagged unless one of those conditions holds. Its category is significance-asserting. `copula-inflation` matches lemma {serve, stand, function, act} + "as" with an inanimate subject, "boast" with an inanimate subject and object, and "holds the distinction of being". Clarity's existing `animacy.py` should exempt person subjects ("She served as treasurer"), and the technical profile should take it at info ("acts as a proxy" is normal there). `that-subject-clause` (csubj with mark "that", 2.6×) belongs as an info-level metric. `vague-association` matches "is/was/became associated with" with a person subject, suppressed when "risk", "increase(d)" or "significantly" occurs nearby, since that is standard medical usage ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). The existing `hedge-stacking` should narrow to same-head redundancy (modal + epistemic adverb on one verb: "may possibly", "could potentially"), because Hyland's 43% clustering is normal science.

The abstract-physical-verb family needs one rule, `abstract-physical-verb` (category false-agency or a new "dead-metaphor" category), with per-lemma shapes. It should reuse the concreteness data already in `src/clarity/data/concreteness.tsv` and the watchlist's `require_concrete_governor` mechanism. It flags when the relevant argument's Brysbaert concreteness falls below a tunable cut-off (start near 3.0 on the 1–5 scale and calibrate; the notes' example values are unverified). Demonstrative subjects (this, that) count as abstract by default. A sense list covers nouns that are concrete in form but used abstractly (point, line, weight, space, shape, engine, thread, lens, seam). A whitelist covers programming and data nouns (variable, field, key, value, column, buffer, register, pointer, array, map, object, function, config, file). Code spans are suppressed.

| Lemma | Flag (metaphorical shape) | Skip (literal shape) | Evidence tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| load-bearing | ADJ or compound on an abstract noun (assumption, sentence, claim, detail) | wall, beam, column, structure | strong: [Arize](https://arize.com/blog/anthropic-says-it-fixed-claudes-writing/), [claudisms.ai](https://claudisms.ai/claudisms.md) |
| do the work / heavy lifting | inanimate or linguistic subject + object lemma work/lifting + "a lot of / most of / all the" | animate subject | strong: Arize, claudisms.ai |
| earn its keep/place/spot | inanimate subject + possessive + {keep, place, spot, seat} | animate subject + money | strong: Arize |

Strong-evidence lemmas can ship at warning. Catalogued ones should ship at info, and the user-observation tier at info with a family-level message ("dead physical metaphor on an abstract subject; say what it does") rather than a per-word claim. A composite (below) promotes density to warning. Orwell's rule 1 justifies the rule for any writer, so it does not depend on proving AI authorship.

### Structural backend: positions, shapes and markup

Structural rules carry the paragraph and document checks that the writing-technique literature supports. `significance-ending` flags a paragraph's last sentence when it holds a significance lexeme or participial tail and no NUM or PROPN token, and reports the document share. Above roughly 30% of paragraphs, the document is templated. The threshold is a guess to calibrate. `topic-sentence-buried` compares sentence 1's content lemmas with the rest of the paragraph. It flags when S1 overlaps with few later sentences while some later Sk overlaps with most. `paragraph-drift` compares content lemmas in the first and second halves of a paragraph (Purdue's unity failure). It uses per-document z-scores rather than fixed cut-offs, following TextTiling's mean − sd policy ([NLTK TextTiling](https://www.nltk.org/_modules/nltk/tokenize/texttiling.html)). `digression-ending` flags a final sentence that introduces entities found nowhere else in the paragraph (Strunk). `missing-link` flags a sentence that shares no content lemma, pronoun or demonstrative with the previous one and carries no connective (Gopen & Swan's logical gap). `additive-glue` flags a sentence-initial additive connective whose sentence has zero content overlap with its predecessor, or two consecutive additive openers. `bare-demonstrative` flags sentence-initial This/These as a pronoun subject, especially after a coordination of three or more items. `dangling-summary-noun` flags "this approach/issue/aspect" when the head lemma is absent from the previous two sentences.

At the section and document level, `heading-body-alignment` flags a section whose heading head noun is absent from the first two sentences. `pre-announcement` flags first-paragraph "In this (article|guide|section) we will (explore|discuss|cover)" and "Let's dive in" (Google's "don't pre-announce"). `colon-reveal` flags "The (result|catch|kicker|irony|takeaway|upshot)?" or the same word followed by ":" and a short fragment. `false-range` flags "from X to Y" with non-numeric, non-date, non-GPE noun phrases, especially paired possessives. `abstract-triad` extends `rule-of-three` to `conj` chains of three where at least two conjuncts are low-concreteness nouns. Markup rules cover several cases. `bold-lead-list` fires when more than half the items in a list match `**Label**:`. `title-case-heading` fires on Markdown headings with more than 70% of non-stopwords capitalised. `emoji-marker` flags emoji at the start of a heading or bullet. `quote-style-mixing` flags curly and straight quotes in one document. `boilerplate-heading` flags Key Takeaways, Final Thoughts, Challenges and Future Directions, Awards and Recognition, and a FAQ placed after a Conclusion. `over-sectioning` fires on sections under about 60 words or headings every fewer than 80 words. Technical procedures add `step-imperative` (a numbered item's first token is VB with no subject), `one-action-per-step` (two or more imperative verbs or sentences), and `condition-first` (an if/when clause after the imperative), all drawn from Google and Microsoft. Academic adds `methods-tense` (more than 50% present-tense finite verbs under a Methods heading) and `methods-hedge` (epistemic markers above about 5 per 1,000 words in Methods, from Hyland's one-tenth figure). Cross-sentence negative parallelism ("isn't X. It's Y.", "Rather, it…" after a negated copula) belongs here too, seeded from the Antislop Appendix L regexes.

### Composite backend: density promotes weak signals to findings

Composites turn individually weak signals into findings that are worth reporting. `challenge-sandwich` requires "Despite (its|their|these) … (face|faces) … challenges" or "Despite these challenges," plus "continues to (thrive|evolve|grow)" or "future … lies in" within a few sentences. It is high precision. `recap-closer` requires a final paragraph opening with In summary / In conclusion / Overall / Ultimately / To sum up, *and* a novelty rate below threshold against the body. Clarity already computes `novelty_rate`, so this scopes it to the closer. `metaphor-density` promotes `abstract-physical-verb` to warning at two or more hits per about 300 words. `ai-lexicon-density` fires at three or more distinct lexicon lemmas within 150 words, which follows Wikipedia's density principle. `generic-paragraph` requires a paragraph of at least N tokens with zero anchors (no NUM, DATE, MONEY, PERCENT, QUANTITY or ORG entities, no PROPN outside the title, no quote or code span) and at least one evaluative lemma (crucial, key, significant, role, impact, landscape, ensure, enhance). The "X plays a (crucial|vital|key) role in" template is a high-precision core. `unquantified-claim` (business, science) requires significantly, substantially, most, many, various, improved or enhanced with no number in the same sentence (Amazon's adjectives-to-data rule). `uncited-claim` (academic) requires "studies show", "research suggests", "it is well known" or "evidence indicates" with no citation marker (bracketed number, Author–Year, superscript) in the same or next sentence. `both-sides-no-verdict` extends the existing `it-depends-conclusion`. It requires balance scaffolding ("On the one hand", "both have strengths and weaknesses", "no one-size-fits-all") in the final section of a comparison document *and* no recommendation verb (use, choose, recommend, prefer) with a named object.

### Document metrics: report first, score after calibration

Clarity's `prose_metrics` block already reports unscored measurements, which suits the population-level signals. Reinhart's effect sizes (d ≈ 0.8–1.4) are real across corpora, but per-document flags on short texts will misfire, and the authors explicitly disclaim detector use ([Reinhart et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v2)). The metrics worth adding, all computable with spaCy, are:

| Metric | Definition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `participial_clause_rate` | VBG advcl/acl per 1k words; flag near 2× a reference rate | 5.3× in GPT-4o |
| `phrasal_coordination_rate` | same-POS NOUN/ADJ/VERB `conj` per 1k | 1.9×; feeds triads |
| `agentless_passive_rate` | `nsubjpass` with no `agent` child, reported as metric not lint | LLMs under-use (~0.5×) |
| `zero_anchor_paragraph_share` | share of paragraphs with no NUM/entity/PROPN/code/quote | within-document genericity |
| `paragraph_shape_entropy` | entropy over (sentence-count bucket, first-sentence type, significance ending, example marker, anchor present) | templated paragraphs |
| `sentences_per_paragraph_cv` | CV of sentence counts | complements existing length distribution |
| `markup_density` | bold + list items + headings per 100 prose tokens; prose-in-bullets share | reward-model format bias |
| `sibling_section_symmetry` | CV of word and paragraph counts across same-level sections; share of 3-item lists | symmetric templates |
| `connective_mix` | additive vs causal vs contrastive vs temporal per 1k; marker type/token | surface glue vs deep cohesion |
| `stance_density` | Hyland hedges + boosters + attitude + self-mention per 1k | low stance + high transitions is the LLM profile |
| `global_cohesion` | content-lemma overlap of each paragraph with neighbours, heading and introduction | the cohesion index that tracks quality |
| `entity_grid_transitions` | S→S share and rough-shift share from noun-chunk heads and roles | focus continuity |
| `claim_recurrence` | (subject, verb, object) lemma triples repeated across non-adjacent sections | paraphrased restatement n-grams miss |
| `number_consistency` | same entity with different numbers | contradiction candidates |
| `idea_density` | CPIDR proposition count ÷ words ([Brown et al. 2008](https://www.ai.uga.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/caspr/BrownSnodgrassKemperHermanCovington2008.pdf)) | filler; norms need recalibration |
| `paragraph_feature_variance` | CV across paragraphs of sentence length, TTR, nominalization, specificity, punctuation mix | burstiness proxy without a language model |

Specificity metrics should follow Li & Nenkova's finding that general sentences have *higher* familiarity and imageability, while concreteness separated the classes only weakly ([Speciteller](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/9517)). Word frequency (wordfreq Zipf), numerals, entities and subjectivity lexicons belong in the specificity score. Clarity's `mean_concreteness` alone will not measure specificity.

## Profiles should become section-aware and genre-sourced

The single most valuable new capability is section awareness: recognising Methods, Discussion, Results and Procedure headings and applying multipliers inside them. Every strong genre number is section-specific, including Hyland's hedge densities, passive voice in Methods and imperatives in procedures. The table gives candidate settings. Each is a starting point in clarity's own "not authority" spirit, with its basis.

Two cross-profile changes follow from the evidence. First, every AI-lexicon entry should carry an era tag (gpt4, gpt4o, gpt5, 2026, claude-opus) so users can weight current tells and retire stale ones. The lists should be versioned and refreshed from the Kobak and Antislop over-representation methods rather than curated by hand. Second, findings should read as writing-quality problems with locations and fixes, never as authorship verdicts. Wikipedia warns that "false accusations of AI use can drive away new editors" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). Detectors have flagged non-native writers' essays as AI-generated at rates up to 61% ([popularai.org, secondary](https://www.popularai.org/p/how-teachers-spot-chatgpt-essays-without-a-detector)).

## Conclusion

The research changes what a slop linter should be built around. Word lists made sense in 2023, when "delve" ran at 28× and models leaked chatbot phrases. By late 2026 the lexicon is turning over each model generation, the em-dash tell has reversed, and phrase lists miss most of what human editors flag. What persists across generations is shape. That means participial significance tails, contrast frames, templated paragraphs that end on importance, additive glue without causal links, generic paragraphs with no anchors, and closers that restate. Clarity's dependency, structural and composite backends can measure these directly, and its existing novelty, concreteness and animacy machinery already supplies most of the inputs. The same rules double as enforcement of classical technique (topic sentences, unity, old-before-new, answer-first, Orwell's dead metaphors). The linter therefore stays useful whoever wrote the draft, and it avoids the false-accusation trap.

Three uncertainties should shape the rollout. The physical-verb family ("holds", "tracks", "sits", "maps to") has strong anecdotal support and almost no measurement, so it should ship at info with density promotion and be calibrated on clarity's own good/bad corpora before it affects the score. Nobody has measured paragraph-level variance, significance-ending rates or zero-anchor shares in matched human and LLM expository prose, so those thresholds are guesses until clarity's `bootstrap` harness measures them. And since copy-editors rate relevance and density above every surface tic, while LLM judges agree with those editors at κ ≈ 0, the most valuable signals are still the hardest to compute. Heading-to-body alignment, global cohesion, claim recurrence and specificity density are the closest deterministic approximations. Combined into a single "substance" score rather than shipped as isolated rules, they are where clarity can do something regex-based linters cannot.
